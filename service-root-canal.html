Root canal treatment

When the nerve inside a tooth is inflamed or infected, root canal treatment cleans it out so the tooth can stay. Here is when it is needed, what happens and why a crown often follows.

Ask on WhatsApp Common questions

At a glance

  • Signs Pain that lingers after hot or cold, throbbing, swelling, or a darkening tooth — sometimes no symptoms at all
  • Visits Usually one to two, occasionally more
  • Anaesthetic Local anaesthetic
  • Afterwards A filling, and for back teeth usually a crown

Typical ranges only — your examination decides what applies to you.

Worth knowing first

  • Antibiotics can calm a flare-up but do not fix the problem inside the tooth.
  • A root-treated tooth is more brittle. Skipping the recommended crown is a common reason these teeth later split.
  • Tenderness when biting for a few days afterwards is normal.
  • Not every tooth can be saved. If the tooth is cracked through or too broken down, we will explain the other options.

What happens, step by step

Every visit starts with an explanation. Nothing begins until you have agreed to it.

01

Diagnosis

We test the tooth and take an X-ray to see the root and whether infection has spread.

02

Cleaning the canals

The tooth is numbed and isolated with a rubber sheet. The inflamed pulp is removed and the canals are cleaned and shaped.

03

Sealing

The canals are filled and sealed. If more than one visit is needed, a temporary filling goes on between visits.

04

Protecting the tooth

A permanent filling closes the tooth. Back teeth usually need a crown so they do not crack under chewing.

Common questions

Is a root canal painful?

With local anaesthetic it usually feels similar to having a filling. The pain people associate with it is often the infection before treatment.

What if I leave it?

The infection does not heal on its own. It can spread into the bone and cause an abscess, and the tooth may eventually be lost.

Why not just pull the tooth?

That is an option, but keeping your own tooth is usually better for chewing and for the teeth around it. A gap may later need an implant, bridge or denture.

Can a root canal fail?

Most treated teeth do well, but sometimes infection returns. The tooth can often be treated again, or other options considered.
