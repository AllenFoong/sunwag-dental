Dental implants

A titanium post replaces the missing root, and a crown sits on top. It takes more than one visit and a few months of healing — here is what that looks like, and what decides whether it suits you.

Ask on WhatsApp Common questions

At a glance

  • Suits One or more missing teeth, with healthy gums and enough jawbone to hold the post
  • Visits Usually three or more: assessment, placement, then the crown
  • Timeline Often around 3–6 months from placement to crown; longer if bone grafting is needed
  • Anaesthetic Local anaesthetic for the placement

Typical ranges only — your examination decides what applies to you.

Worth knowing first

  • Not enough bone? A bone graft may be needed first, which adds time.
  • Smoking and poorly controlled diabetes raise the chance of an implant not settling in. Tell us about both.
  • An implant cannot decay, but the gum around it can still get infected. It needs brushing, flossing and check-ups like any tooth.
  • A bridge or a denture can also replace a missing tooth. We explain how they compare for your case.

What happens, step by step

Every visit starts with an explanation. Nothing begins until you have agreed to it.

01

Assessment

We look at the gap, your gums and your general health, and arrange X-ray imaging to check the bone.

02

Placing the post

Under local anaesthetic the post is placed in the jawbone and the gum is closed over or around it.

03

Healing

Bone grows onto the post over a few months. A temporary tooth can sometimes fill the gap meanwhile.

04

Abutment and crown

Once it is firm, the connector and a crown shaded to match your teeth are fitted.

Common questions

Does it hurt?

The area is numbed, so you should feel pressure rather than pain during placement. Soreness and some swelling for a few days afterwards is common and usually managed with ordinary painkillers.

How long does an implant last?

With good cleaning and regular check-ups, implants often last many years. The crown on top may wear and need replacing at some point.

Am I too old for an implant?

Age on its own is rarely the deciding factor. Bone, gum health and general health matter more — that is what the assessment is for.

Can the implant go in the same day a tooth is pulled?

Sometimes, if the site is healthy and the bone is suitable. Often it is better to let the socket heal first. We decide after examining it.
