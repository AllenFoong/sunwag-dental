Braces

Fixed braces move teeth with small brackets and a wire that is adjusted over time. Here is what the process involves, how long it tends to take and what daily life with braces is like.

Ask on WhatsApp Common questions

At a glance

  • Suits Crowding, gaps, crooked teeth and many bite problems, in teens and adults
  • Duration Commonly around 1–2 years, depending on how much needs to move
  • Visits Adjustments roughly every 4–8 weeks
  • Afterwards A retainer to hold the teeth in their new position

Typical ranges only — your examination decides what applies to you.

Worth knowing first

  • Teeth usually feel sore for a few days after fitting and after adjustments.
  • Brushing takes longer. Food trapped around brackets can lead to decay and marks on the teeth if it is not cleaned well.
  • Hard and sticky foods can break brackets — nuts, hard sweets, chewing gum.
  • Teeth tend to drift back without a retainer, so wearing it as advised is part of the treatment.

What happens, step by step

Every visit starts with an explanation. Nothing begins until you have agreed to it.

01

Consultation

You tell us what you want to change. We examine your teeth and bite and take records such as photos and X-rays.

02

Treatment plan

We explain the options, whether any teeth need to come out for space, and roughly how long it will take.

03

Fitting the braces

Brackets are bonded to the teeth and the wire is placed. It takes one longer visit and does not need an injection.

04

Adjustments and retainer

Regular visits to adjust the wire. When the teeth are in place the braces come off and a retainer takes over.

Common questions

Am I too old for braces?

Adults can have braces as long as the teeth and gums are healthy. Treatment may take a little longer than in teenagers.

Will teeth need to be pulled?

Not always. If there is not enough room, removing some teeth may be one option. We explain why before anything is decided.

Braces or clear aligners?

Both can work for many cases. Braces stay on all the time; aligners are removable but only work if worn 20–22 hours a day. Some complex movements suit braces better.

Can I play sport or an instrument?

Yes. A mouthguard is sensible for contact sports, and wind instruments take a little time to get used to.
